# 02 — Creative directions

Four directions were developed far enough to judge. They differ in brand strategy, composition, imagery, type and interaction, not just colour.

## A. "Sillage": the invisible, drawn
**Strategy:** scent is invisible, so draw it. Every fragrance gets a generated *signature*: contour lines computed from its real top, heart and base notes, like a topographic map of how the scent moves through a room.
**Composition:** editorial, generous margins, signatures at poster scale.
**Imagery:** almost none needed; the signatures are the imagery. **Type:** editorial serif with a precise grotesk.
**Interaction:** a scent map that places all 67 fragrances by character.
**Risk:** can tip into abstract art; Hospitality and Technology need their own expression.

## B. "Plan & section": the architect's drawing set
**Strategy:** Global DWC as a spatial-design partner. Floor plans, sections and zone diagrams on travertine paper.
**Interaction:** drop diffusers into a lobby plan and see coverage.
**Risk:** coverage figures are not published, so the core interaction would invent data. It's also cold, and the least likely to excite younger visitors.

## C. "Dubai hours": time-of-day cinema
**Strategy:** the site follows Dubai time (dawn, noon, dusk, night) with palette and copy shifting, framed as "scent programmed by daypart".
**Imagery:** photography-led.
**Risk:** depends on commissioned photography that doesn't exist; the gimmick wears off on the second visit; the time-based claims are not verified.

## D. "Index": the catalogue as the brand
**Strategy:** a precise, Swiss-style index of everything the group sells (67 scents, 14 diffusers, 11 hospitality and technology categories) with codes, prices and states.
**Interaction:** fast filtering, keyboard-first.
**Risk:** credible and useful, but on its own it reads as a parts catalogue, not a sensory brand.

## Selected: A inside D — **"Lines of atmosphere"**

The two strongest directions fix each other's weakness. **Sillage** brings the emotion and the ownable brand device; **Index** brings the credibility and the shop-floor usefulness. Neither depends on invented facts or photography the company doesn't have.

### The idea
> One line language, three behaviours.

Everything is drawn with the same 1 px line on warm limestone paper:
- **Aroma** — contour lines that flow (sillage). Every scent's signature is computed from its notes.
- **Hospitality** — lines that weave (warp and weft: linen, order, care).
- **Technology** — lines that switch (signal steps: precise, connected).

That gives the group one recognisable identity while each division still feels distinct. It works on a phone, on a printed sample card and in an Instagram post.

### Why it's right for Global Aroma
- **It fixes a real problem.** Every oil in the store shares one placeholder image. Signatures give each of the 67 fragrances its own image, generated from its own data, at zero photography cost.
- **It uses real data.** Notes, families, prices and product links come from the official site (Sonora's lesson: show the real thing).
- **It reads to both audiences.** A procurement manager sees a calm, ordered catalogue with prices and clear routes to buy or ask; a younger visitor sees a generative visual system that changes with every scent.

### Identity system
- **Paper** `#F2EEE7` (limestone), **Ink** `#1A1714` (warm black). Greys are ink at opacity.
- **Division accents** (used as small signals only): Aroma *Saffron* `#B4612A`, Hospitality *Juniper* `#3E5C50`, Technology *Lapis* `#3149B0`.
- **Scent family pigments** (signatures only): citrus, green, marine, floral, woody, oriental and gourmand. Muted, mineral, like dry pigments.
- **Type:** *Newsreader* (optical-size serif) for statements and scent names, roman by default, never decorative italics. *Schibsted Grotesk* for UI and body. *JetBrains Mono* for codes, prices and labels.
- **Rules:** 1 px lines, square corners, hard 12-column grid, no gradients, no glows, no stock statistics. Motion is slow and optional.

### The signature interaction: the Scent Map → Brief
A commercial buyer's real question is "what should our lobby smell like, and what do we buy?" The site answers it in three moves:
1. **Map:** all 67 real fragrances plotted by character (fresh ↔ warm, light ↔ deep), computed from their notes. Drag or tap to explore; each opens its note pyramid and signature.
2. **Shortlist:** add scents, diffusers, hospitality or technology categories to a **Brief** from any page. It persists on the device.
3. **Send:** the Brief becomes a structured enquiry by email or WhatsApp to verified company contacts. Anything sold online links straight to the existing WooCommerce product.

It's functional, not decorative: it replaces the broken contact form and the leftover electronics catalogue with one clear path.

### Information architecture
`/` home · `/aroma` (library, map, diffusers, home fragrance, sampler, custom) · `/hospitality` · `/technology` · `/brief` (enquiry builder) · `/contact` · `/studio` (unlisted social-asset generator). Six public routes plus the studio: fewer than the old 11-item menu, but covering all three divisions.
